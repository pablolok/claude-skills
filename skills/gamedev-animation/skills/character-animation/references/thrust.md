# One-handed spear thrust at a low target

[S] = a source's number, [E] = a derived or design value. A 1.8 m man, a spear in the right hand. The left hand is
free or carries a light source (a torch, a lantern).

## Grip and carry

**Overhand or underhand.** Period art shows mostly overhand. Reenactor tests found underhand or low thrusts more
accurate and less tiring, with a longer effective reach; the reach claim is disputed. Keep overhand for downward
blows over shields or at high targets. In spear-and-shield practice the grip is loose and hip rotation drives the
force along the shaft.

**For a target 0.3-0.5 m high, 1.5-2.5 m away [E]:**
- Overhand (hand ~1.55 m high, ~1.2 m of shaft ahead) must point the shaft ~70° down. That makes an ice-pick stab
  reaching only ~1.25 m.
- **Underhand** (hand ~0.85 m at the impact, shaft ~20-25° down) lands the point ~2.2 m ahead, and keeps the spear's
  line clear of a raised off hand.

**Carry [E].**
- A ~2.0 m spear held ~0.8 m from the butt (1.2 m ahead).
- Guard hand at the right hip (~0.95 m high, 0.15 m out), elbow 100-110°.
- The butt tucked behind the forearm, under the elbow. The point 0.7-0.9 m high on the target line.
- Forearm neutral (thumb toward the point), ulnar deviation ≤ 20°.

**Hold the spear diagonally**, ~52° across the palm (check this). In a plain hammer grip it sits ~60° off the
forearm, and no wrist aims it down a thrust's line (aim errors of 20-30°, the wrist pinned at its limit).

## Biomechanics

**Energy and order.** In a rear-hand straight punch the energy comes from rear-leg extension 38 %, trunk rotation
37 % and arm extension 24 % [S]. The order:
1. The rear leg pushes.
2. The pelvis turns while the chest still points away.
3. The front leg lands stiff and brakes.

Peak speeds run hip → shoulder → elbow → wrist → hand [S].

**Hand before foot.** Elite fencers start the arm before the front foot [S]. In animation the arm leads and the foot
lands just before or at the hit.

**The lunge.**
- A fencing lunge is 1.17-1.26 m. The COM travels 0.40 m in the acceleration phase at ~1.7 m/s [S].
- An untrained soldier does ~60 % of that [E]: a step of 0.55-0.65 m, the feet ending 0.9-1.0 m apart, the pelvis
  travelling 0.35-0.40 m.

**Joints in a fencing lunge [S].**
- Front knee: 48° at heel-off → 24° at heel strike. It lands nearly straight, heel first, then bends to absorb
  (45-60° [E]).
- Front hip: 40° → 62°.
- Rear knee: 40° → 14°, ending near straight (175-177° included).
- Trunk: 21-43° forward lean [S]; 20-25° for a low target [E].

**Trunk turn [E].** Pelvis ~35° and chest ~50° from the chamber to the impact. In the chamber the chest is ~15°
further away.

**The elbow does not lock.** Elite fencers reach ~177°. Use 160-170° at the impact and +2-5° in the settle.

**Speeds.** Fist peak 6.6-12.5 m/s, 8-21 ms before contact [S]. A fencing blade 2.5-2.9 m/s [S]. A spear hand ~5-7
m/s [E].

**A thrust's chain is LINEAR.** The forearm keeps its direction and travels, so compare the joints' linear speeds,
not their angular ones. The fist's fastest frame is the one before the contact.

**The butt passes OUTSIDE the ribs.** Put the hand a little right of the line, and turn the shaft left as far as
the wrist goes. A hand key the arm cannot reach (full extension) jolts the spear sideways: key where it reaches.

## Timing (24 fps, damage at 0.8 s)

A lunge takes 0.54 s of movement and a jab 0.40 s [S]. A telegraph needs reaction (0.25 s) + dodge ≈ 18 frames at
30 fps [S].

| frames | phase | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| f0-2 | cue | the head locks on; the off hand lifts |
| f2-10 | chamber | spear back 0.20-0.25 m behind the hip, elbow 85-95°, chest away 25-30°, 70 % weight on the rear leg, knees 25°, the point on the target line |
| f10-13 | hold | the chamber reads |
| f13 | drive | the rear heel pushes; the front foot lifts |
| f14-15 | pelvis | the pelvis turns |
| f15-16 | trunk | the trunk turns |
| f16-18 | arm | the arm extends |
| f18 | landing | the front heel lands |
| f19 | impact | elbow 165°, arm 40-45° below horizontal, hand ~1.1 m ahead and 0.85 m high, point ~2.2 m ahead |
| f19-22 | stick | the front knee sinks 10-15°, the tip dips 3-5 cm, a 2-frame hold |
| f22-28 | recoil | the spear snaps back 0.3-0.4 m |
| f28-40 | recovery | the front foot comes back; settle into guard |

Wind up at full speed, accelerate into the strike, and decelerate after contact. Enemies get a longer anticipation
than the player.

## Real vs fake

**Fake**:
- an arm-only poke;
- the elbow snapping to 180°;
- the point wobbling off its line (the point should travel straight, with the butt tracking behind the forearm);
- twinning;
- no weight shift;
- the recovery played as the strike reversed.

**From a high camera**:
- The chamber reads as the spear pulled back along its own axis, plus the body coiling away (~0.25 m).
- At the impact: a long diagonal line from the off hand to the point, and a long stride.
- A hitch in the chamber: a key pose held ~3 frames.

## The off arm (carrying a light)

A fencer's rear arm is thrown back as a counterweight [S]. A light source must stay up [E].
- **Guard**: elbow 90-110°, the light's head 1.7-1.9 m high, 0.3-0.4 m out to the left-front, ≥ 0.3 m from the
  face.
- **Chamber**: forward 0.15-0.2 m. It lights the target and balances the spear drawn back.
- **Strike**: back and up 0.2-0.3 m. It counters the chest's turn.
- Never below the shoulder.

# A one-handed sword blow — overhead or diagonal

Right-handed, a one-handed sword, optionally a shield on the left arm.

## Key poses

- **Guard**: feet about hip-width apart (not wider: the body must be able to put its weight over one foot), knees
  soft, the weight centred.
- **Wind-up**:
  - The fist is over the right side of the head, the elbow up and out.
  - The blade is laid back ~45° behind the head.
  - The trunk is turned away and the weight settles back. Blade behind the head means the forearm is up and back.
- **Action**: the front foot steps (a passing step or a lunge, driven from the hips) and the knees bend. The fist
  leads over the top with the blade LAGGING, then the blade whips over.
- **Impact**:
  - The arm is nearly straight, long and forward at chest height.
  - The blade is angled down from the arm; it cannot continue the arm's line in a hammer grip.
  - The forearm hangs forward-down with the wrist snapped ulnar.
  - The blade goes down and across, right shoulder to left hip.
  - With the fist low, the blade points back through the front thigh. Keep the fist at chest height.
- **Follow-through**: across to the opposite hip. The body brakes and the blade carries on.
- **Counterweight**: the shield (or the off arm) comes forward.

## The whip

1. The arm comes down first, with the wrist still cocked and the blade lagging, pointing up.
2. The arm brakes.
3. The wrist snaps the blade through in the last frame.

Measured on one baked swing at 24 fps (check this):
- Peak frames of the chain: hips 26 → chest 27 → upper arm 27 → forearm 28 → hand 28.
- Tip speed: 6 → 13 → 21 → 26 → 25 m/s into the impact, then 10.5 → 7.2 → 7.4 across to the left.

Move the elbow's pole over several frames. Flipped in one frame, it rolls the whole arm 50-70° in that frame.

## Timing

- A heavy overhead blow with its recovery runs about 40 frames at 24 fps (1.7 s) (check this).
- Hold the wind-up's top for a moving hold of ≥ 3 frames. It is the enemy's telegraph: ≥ 0.3 s from the first tell
  to the impact.
- Measured chain on such a blow: hips 12 → chest 13 → upper arm 13 → forearm 14 → hand 14 (check this).

## Stepping out of a lunge (the recovery)

- The rear heel comes down BEFORE the weight comes back.
- The pelvis goes back slowly. Going back fast, the XCoM (`COM + v/ω0`) overshoots the rear heel: at 0.7 m/s by
  ~9 cm (check this).
- The front foot rolls onto its ball while the weight leaves it; from over the rear foot the leg cannot reach it
  flat. It steps when the moving body is over the rear foot.
- It returns to guard only once that foot is down.

**Capture-point stepping.** With the front foot off, hold the XCoM over the rear foot: the COM closes in
exponentially (time constant `1/ω0 ≈ 0.31 s`).
- The front leg pushes first (~1.3 m/s back).
- The foot lifts as the XCoM reaches the rear foot.
- The rear heel stays up until the pelvis comes back over it.
- Hand-placed keys overshoot the rear heel; solve this phase instead of keying it.

## Fake

- The arm alone; the legs standing still.
- The blade aimed straight down the forearm.
- A blade that stops dead at the impact.
- The elbow pole flipping in a frame.
- The recovery played as the strike reversed.
- A wide body or armour pushing the sword arm into the ribs. Give the bake a "room" offset that keeps the hand in
  front where it crosses a wider chest, and measure it against a bake without it.

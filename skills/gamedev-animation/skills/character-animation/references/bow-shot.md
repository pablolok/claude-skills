# Longbow shot

A right-handed archer: the bow in the LEFT hand, the left side toward the target.
- (S) = a source's number.
- (D) = derived for a 1.8 m man: shoulder ≈ 1.47 m, jaw ≈ 1.57, mouth corner ≈ 1.62, ear ≈ 1.65; upper arm 0.335,
  elbow → string fingers ≈ 0.42.

## Key poses

**Stance.**
- Modern: feet parallel or slightly open, no wider than the shoulders, knees soft; 60-70 % of the weight on the
  balls (S).
- Medieval or heavy bow:
  - Feet at least shoulder-width apart (~0.40-0.50 m, D), the front (left) foot angled forward.
  - The bow-side leg heavily bent and the arrow-side leg straight.
  - "In the bow": hips back, sitting in the draw, weight onto the front leg, a hollow back.
  - The upper body's tilt is the bow's tilt. A higher trajectory comes from the hips and legs, not the arms (S).

**The five phases** (period teaching): standing, nocking, drawing, holding, loosing.

**Set-up.** Both arms rise without lifting the shoulders. The draw hand starts back at about eye level, and the bow
arm may go slightly above the target line (S). A warbow draw starts with the arrow down or level, pushing the bow
and pulling the string together, up to just over the target.

**Bow arm (L) at full draw.**
- Shoulder abduction ~90° (elite 99 ± 4.5°, S).
- The elbow slightly bent, never locked (156-158° included, S), and rotated so the string clears the forearm.
- The bow shoulder DOWN.
- The load on the pad below the thumb, with hand, wrist, elbow and shoulder in one line, "leaning on a wall" (S).
- The grip ~1.42-1.48 m high, ~0.62-0.66 m from the bow shoulder (D).

**Draw arm (R).**
- Shoulder abduction ~99°.
- Elbow flexion 138 ± 5° (elite) vs 144° (novice), i.e. 36-45° included (S).
- The elbow level with the arrow or 1-2 cm above it, behind or past the arrow's line; no "triangulation" (S).
- The draw hand travels along the face into the anchor while the head stays still: the string comes to the face.
- Geometry (D): draw shoulder → anchor ≈ 0.26-0.30 m, the elbow 0.30-0.35 m behind the anchor. The bow arm is
  therefore horizontally adducted ~10-20° from the shoulder line.

**Back.** "Opening the bow": the body turns about the bow shoulder. The draw scapula goes down and toward the spine
(lower trapezius), and the load splits 50:50 left/right (S). Closing the shoulder blades draws the bow; pulling the
string back with the arm alone does not.

**Anchor.**
- Longbow: "three under" at the mouth corner (~1.62 m, D).
- Warbow: no face anchor. It is drawn until the head touches the bow hand, the hand ending by the ear (~1.62-1.66 m).

**Draw length.** 28-29 in (0.71-0.74 m) for 1.8 m (D). Period war arrows are ~30 in, and a warbow draw is up to
32 in. The force line runs from the nock to the bow hand's pressure point.

**Head.** Turned to the target and still, with no tilt toward the string (S).

## Timing (448 recurve shots, S)

| phase | time |
|---|---|
| set-up | 2.17 ± 0.68 s |
| draw | 2.51 ± 0.77 s (0.92-5.09) |
| aim | 3.49 ± 1.73 s |
| clicker → release | 173 ms |
| follow-through | 1.34 ± 0.40 s |

- The string leaves the fingers in < 16 ms. Postural adjustments come 30-130 ms before release.
- Hold ≤ 1-3 s; period teaching warns against a long hold.
- The war rate is ~10-12 shots/min. A warbow draw is one continuous push-pull, so compressing it to a 1.0 s
  telegraph is fair.

## Frame plan (24 fps, release at f25)

| frames | phase | what happens |
|---|---|---|
| F1-4 | tell | the head snaps to the target, the weight goes onto the front leg, the bow is low, the arrow down |
| F4-9 | raise | the bow arm to 85-95°, a little above the line; the draw hand to eye level, ~0.25 m in front of the face |
| F9-19 | draw | eased out into the anchor as the bow gets heavier: push and pull together, the draw elbow arcs back and up, the scapula retracts, the trunk leans into the bow 5-10°, hips back |
| F19-22 | anchor | the full-draw pose reads |
| F22-25 | expansion | 1-2 cm more, the elbow creeping back (2-3 mm in reality) |
| F25 | release | the fingers open |
| F25-28 | snap | the draw hand recoils 5-10 cm back along the jaw and neck, ending below or behind the ear; the bow jumps forward and down; the bow wrist and bow tip drop 10-20°; the bow arm stays up |
| F28-38 | follow-through | a hold (0.4 s) |
| F38-52 | recovery | the bow arm to 30-45°, the draw hand to the quiver, the weight re-centres |

## Measured rules

**Side-on without a step.**
- Both feet turn on their balls with the heels up, the weight forward over the balls while they turn. Exclude the
  turning soles from the foot-slip check.
- The pelvis turns ~50° and the chest ~38° more.

**Compare with the coach's views.** Render the same views a coach uses (straight down, and from the chest's front)
and set them side by side with photos. The checks:
- the shoulders ON the arrow's line (the chest side-on, 90° to the target);
- the head turned fully to look down the arrow;
- the draw hand under the jaw, the forearm across the jaw with the eyes and nose clear;
- the draw elbow behind the head at the arrow's height, in its vertical plane.

A chest turned only part of the way, with an open stance, puts the draw elbow out to the side.

**Draw checks.**
- The draw length.
- The arrow against the aim.
- The shoulders against the arrow: < 15°.
- The draw elbow over the arrow's line by 0-6 cm, and < 6 cm out of its plane.
- The bow arm at ~80-90° abduction and ~152-158° included.
- The bow hand still: < 1 cm/frame.
- At the release, the draw hand goes back ~7 cm and the bow hand drops ~4-5 cm.

**The draw goes from the bow toward the face** (`anchor + line · d`, with d shrinking). With the sign wrong, the
draw hand passes behind the anchor and comes back: check the hand never goes past it.

**Measure the anchor, don't guess it.** Bake once, then read the skull: the jaw's corner, offset toward the face,
down and to the side. A guessed anchor puts the fingers into the face; one at the mouth lets the forearm cover the
nose.

**The draw hand is at the limit of supination.** Knuckles upright with the elbow behind is ~90° from the forearm's
neutral, past the ±80° range.
- Allow ~95° for this clip, and cant the hook ~40° (check this).
- Turn the draw elbow up and back only once the hand is at eye level. Turned earlier, the forearm's twist is left
  behind and the hand rolls far off its aim.

**Short arms give a short draw.** A short-armed body gets a shorter draw and a more bent draw elbow. That is the
proportions, not the pose.

**Pivot a foot with BOTH feet down.** With the toes up on one heel, the support is a heel. Step first, pivot after,
and key a pivot every frame.

## Fake

- drawing with the arm only (scapula and trunk static);
- a locked or collapsed bow arm;
- a hunched bow shoulder;
- the draw hand short of the face, at the chest, or floating;
- the draw elbow low or cocked high;
- the head leaning to the string;
- a linear, constant-speed draw;
- a statue hold;
- a forward release or a wide fling;
- no weight shift;
- the string across the forearm;
- canting from the wrist.

## Longbow specifics

- Cant from the upper body and hips, never the wrist: the bow vertical, or 5-10° from the trunk (D).
- To track a moving target, keep the T (bow hand, shoulders, draw elbow) and turn the whole upper body at the hips
  (S). Allow ±30° of trunk/hip yaw while drawing; beyond that, replant the feet (D).

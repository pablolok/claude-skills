# Player walkthrough and playability

## Select a perspective

Choose one profile per pass and state assumptions about prior knowledge, input skill, patience, and goals.
Useful profiles include first-time, returning, invested, optimizer, genre-experienced, or spectator-focused.
Use custom profiles for accessibility, device, or social context. Do not treat a profile as a population statistic.

Maintain a small emotion vocabulary for comparison: curious, engaged, delighted, empowered, proud,
connected, neutral, contemplative, focused, tense, relieved, confused, frustrated, bored, overwhelmed, cheated.
Add an explicit "understands" marker for a learning breakthrough. These labels organize observations;
they do not measure another person's internal state without supporting evidence.

## Walk through relevant phases

1. First contact: identity, first interactive element, time to meaningful action.
2. Learning: immediate goal, controls, first successful use, first failure, recovery, first choice.
3. Core session: repetition, variation, earned reward, peaks/rests, natural stopping point.
4. Return: remembered controls, repeated prompts/scenes, new depth, newly visible friction.
5. Mastery: strategic options, self-directed goals, optimization, routine versus growth.

Do not impose run/session lengths on games with different structures. Choose checkpoints around actual
decisions and transitions. Record time to understanding rather than prescribing a universal minute mark.

```text
Profile and prior knowledge: [assumptions]
Evidence mode: [observed tester / reviewer play / simulated walkthrough]
Build and session: [identifier, first/repeat/mastery]
Time | Visible event | Player action/inference | Emotion/evidence | Flag | Note
Flags: working / friction / serious risk / unknown

First failure and recovery: [cause understood? retry cost?]
Peak: [specific moment and why]
Reason to continue or return: [observed statement or explicit hypothesis]
Blind spots: [missing behavior or evidence]
```

Look for confusion turning into understanding, tension followed by relief, and failure leading to a revised plan.
Investigate sustained frustration, repeated overload, unexplained loss of earned skill, and long neutral stretches.
Support severity with behavior and context rather than guessing that every quiet moment causes quitting.

## Playability rubric /12

Score each 0-2: 0 missing/broken, 1 partial or inconsistent, 2 demonstrated for the declared scope/profile.

| Dimension | Evidence for 2 |
|---|---|
| Loop closure | The central action produces a meaningful outcome that changes or motivates the next action |
| Session viability | The intended session contains varied, sustainable play and a suitable stopping point |
| Onboarding clarity | Goal, controls, and central action are understood without undisclosed assistance |
| Failure recovery | The player can understand failure and resume or adapt at an appropriate cost |
| Continuation motive | A visible next goal, curiosity, mastery, or reward motivates further play |
| Memorable peak | A specific moment expresses the intended experience strongly enough to recall |

Use 10-12 as a review-ready signal, 7-9 for targeted revision, 4-6 for major gaps, and 0-3 for
an incomplete playable experience. Judge scope and production stage explicitly; no total cancels a softlock.
Do not label long-term retention demonstrated from a short walkthrough.

For repeat-play claims, revisit early, familiar, and mastered states. Report what changed and what became friction.
When comparing builds, preserve profile, start state, settings, and scoring definitions.

# Quality checks by channel

Apply relevant checks at the final game camera and delivery scale. Record intentional exceptions.

## Light and value

- Establish a dominant light/value hierarchy and one primary focal area.
- Separate important subjects from their backgrounds by value, hue, edge, shape, or focus.
- Check foreground/midground/background organization where depth carries meaning.
- Keep required shadow information readable; use black deliberately rather than banning it.
- Verify motivated practicals, stable exposure, and continuity between related views.
- Inspect both greyscale and colour, including bright and dark display conditions.
- Use grade and atmosphere to support hierarchy rather than flattening every object into one tone.

## Atmosphere

- Choose fog, scattering, dust, weather, and air movement for the place and intended mood.
- Check depth behavior and intersections; a flat additive overlay may fail to describe volume.
- Preserve navigation, silhouettes, and threat cues through atmospheric effects.
- Reserve stillness when it contributes to a pause or contrast; motion in every frame is not required.

## Matter and set dressing

- Check silhouette and proportions against the concept at equal viewing scale.
- Where the style uses physical lighting, inspect roughness, normals, metal response, transparency,
  and emission as well as base colour. For other styles, inspect their corresponding surface rules.
- Check tiling, texture density, edge quality, seams, contact, floating pieces, and intersections.
- Dress large forms before medium props and small detail; preserve the route and action space.
- Make wear, damage, clutter, and material transitions tell a coherent spatial story.
- Check this: cloth tears represented as rigid spikes may read like shards. Compare the silhouette,
  opacity, and deformation to the intended fabric before choosing a geometry or texture solution.
- Validate final maps and lighting; a clay preview establishes geometry only.

## Character and motion

- Ensure required states exist: rest, movement, action, reaction, and failure/exit where applicable.
- Match translation to locomotion and contact; check sliding, penetration, planted feet, and support.
- Structure actions through anticipation, execution, contact/event, and recovery.
- Place the gameplay event on the visible contact or explain any purposeful offset.
- Give dialogue and reactions a pose, attention target, and readable hold.
- Check this: a single curve applied to all facial controls can make expression changes feel rigid.
  Test staggered feature timing, asymmetry, gaze, and breath where the performance calls for them.
- Use blink and facial timing appropriate to the anatomy and style; fixed human measurements are not universal.
  For a human face: the upper lid does almost all of a blink's travel, the lower lid moves about 2 mm at most; the
  lid closes in about 100 ms and opens more slowly, in about 150–200 ms. A blink with both lids travelling alike, or
  closing and opening at one speed, reads mechanical.
- Check interacting bodies through the final camera, including differences in target size and pose.

## Effects

- Make effects describe source, direction, impact, scale, and consequence.
- Match the effect vocabulary to the action: a melee attack should not imply a different weapon or mechanism.
- Inspect particle intersections, sorting, temporal stability, overdraw, trails, and fade-out.
- Verify the effect's absence does not conceal broken animation or contact.
- Offer reduced flash/motion where needed and retain the gameplay information under those settings.

## Sound

- Inventory player actions, impacts, movement, interactions, UI, threats, and narrative beats.
- Give each an intentional sound state, including deliberate silence.
- Align transients with visible events; verify perceived source and distance.
- Use variation when repeated playback causes fatigue, while preserving learnable meaning.
- Establish priorities for speech, threats, feedback, ambience, and music; check masking and ducking.
- Check room response, transitions, loops, clipping, missing tails, and device playback.
- Check this: a delivered clip may play much quieter than the review monitor. Verify exported level,
  channels, and audio presence on the intended playback path before drawing a design conclusion.

## Feel and camera

- Measure input acknowledgment separately from intentional windup or commitment.
- Check feedback synchronization across visual, audio, camera/haptic, and target reaction channels.
- Tune event importance so routine actions do not compete with major consequences.
- Honor shake, flash, camera-motion, and input settings; preserve clarity when effects are reduced.
- Keep gameplay subjects visible through occlusion, look-ahead, and camera motion.
- Choose cinematic framing, focus, and movement for a named purpose; handheld treatment is optional.
- Verify takeovers, blends, skip, and the first playable input after a cinematic.

## UI and words

- Check purpose, hierarchy, grouping, spacing, typography, focus, and a clear next action.
- Compare the rendered object and its joins to the chosen concept, not only bounding boxes and colours.
- Check text at the intended viewing distance, worst background, localized length, and user scale.
- Verify keyboard/gamepad/pointer behavior as applicable, visible focus, remapping, and feedback states.
- Keep overlapping messages and reading demands under control.
- Check canon, voice, knowledge, and terminology against the story model.
- Ensure lore and required information reach the player through an actual game channel.

## Play and production finish

- Check meaningful decisions, enemy roles, readable punish/recovery windows, and intended progression.
- Judge depth in terms of the host game's loop rather than requiring run-based progression.
- Validate settings, save/load boundaries, supported input devices, and target build behavior.
- Check first-use stalls, shader/asset loading, frame-time spikes, and audio interruptions.
- Confirm third-party asset permissions through the host project's asset process.
- Log remaining gaps instead of calling missing functionality polish.

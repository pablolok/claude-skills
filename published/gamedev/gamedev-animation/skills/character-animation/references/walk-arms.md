# The arms in a walk — the hang, the swing, the relaxed hand

Empty hands hang relaxed. A hand that holds something (a chain, a ring of keys) has its own pose.

A typical failure looks like this:
- a nearly straight arm swung ±12° in one plane;
- the arm angled out from the body;
- the hands fanned well off the thighs.

That reads stiff and A-posed.

## 1. The numbers (healthy adults, comfortable to fast walking)

| | value |
|---|---|
| shoulder flexion-extension range | 25° (0.83 m/s) → 30° (1.0 m/s); faster walking swings more |
| shoulder abduction range | 6-8°: the arm is not a pure sagittal pendulum |
| shoulder **rotation** range | 17-22°: the humerus turns in and out through the swing |
| shoulder rotation's mean | internal through most of the swing |
| elbow range | 15-20°, up to ~30° in some studies |
| elbow **mean** flexion | ~40°: the elbow is never near straight |
| timing | the arm swings opposite its leg, its forward peak near the other heel's strike; the elbow is most bent with the forward swing (both rise in 40-75 % of the cycle) and straightest at the back |
| relaxed hand | the **cascade**: fingers in slight flexion, the little finger most and the index least, ~70-90° summed along a finger; the thumb relaxed beside the index, not across the palm |

## 2. What photos show on a heavy, muscular man

- **The hang.** The upper arm is pushed out ~10-15° by the lats and the arm's own bulk. The elbow is bent and the
  forearm comes back in, so the hand hangs at the side of the thigh, a hand's width off it. The whole arm is NOT
  angled out in one straight line; that reads as an A-pose.
- **The shoulders sit forward** (protracted, the traps heavy). The arm hangs a little in front of the trunk's line.
  The palm faces the thigh or turns slightly back (the internal rotation above), and the thumb points forward.
- **The hand** is limp, the fingers loosely curled in the cascade with the tips well off the palm. Never fanned,
  never a fist.

## 3. How animators make it read

- **The upper arm drives.** The forearm and the hand follow a beat late (overlap). The hand trails at each change
  of direction: the wrist breaks back at the start of the forward swing and forward at the start of the back swing.
  It does not move rigidly with the forearm.
- **Arcs, not a line.** In front, the hand swings slightly toward the midline (the humerus turning in); behind,
  slightly out. From the game camera the path is a shallow curve.
- **The girdle moves with the arm.**
  - The shoulder blade slides forward with the forward swing and back with the back swing (a few degrees of
    clavicle protraction and retraction).
  - The shoulders counter-rotate the pelvis.
  - The shoulder line rides the trunk's side sway and bob. A heavy body sways side to side more than it twists.
  - A shoulder that only hinges reads stiff.
- **A heavy body's arms** make a slower, damped pass with no snap at the ends. The swing's size follows the walking
  speed: too small for the stride reads stiff, too big reads light.

## 4. Checks for a walking arm

- **Elbow**: its mean flexion and range against §1. For a body whose flesh stops it earlier, its measured range wins.
- **Shoulder rotation**: not zero through the cycle. The hand's path from the game camera curves inward in front.
- **Lag**: the forearm and the hand lag the upper arm (compare the phase of each segment's peak), and the wrist's own
  turn reverses at each change of direction.
- **Hand**:
  - From the game camera, no finger sits apart from the others like a fan.
  - The hand's gap to the thigh is ≥ 0, and close to a hand's width at the passing pose.
- **Girdle**: the clavicle's protraction follows the arm's swing in phase, by a few degrees. The armpit and the
  deltoid read clean from the game camera at both extremes.
- **High-camera projection**: from a high game camera, a long arm's hanging hand projects onto the knee whatever the
  swing. Only the hang's height, or a smaller swing, changes that. Present it as a choice early (check this).

## 5. On a generated body

- **The hand's fan comes from the bind pose.** A body bound with spread fingers keeps a fan under any curl. Bring the
  spread toward zero first, then add the cascade.
- **Classify a bent finger's crossings before fixing them.** The palm folding onto itself at a knuckle is contact,
  not breakage.
- **Read a joint's clean range on every neighbouring region**, not on its own alone.
